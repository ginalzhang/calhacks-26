## Proposed authoritative plan text

Proposed replacement:

> Use Apple `SpeechAnalyzer` and `SpeechTranscriber` with `en-US`, `fastResults`, and audio-time attributes for on-device transcription. Use the pinned, bundled libfvad WebRTC VAD exclusively for speech/silence boundaries; do not depend on `SpeechDetector.results`. Apple speech-asset installation remains explicit setup; subsequent transcription and VAD operation must work offline. Tentative text never causes an action.
>
> Process contiguous 10 ms VAD frames in the same audio-time domain as the analyzer. An utterance starts at the first speech-classified frame. Its detected speech end is the end of the latest speech-classified frame. Close the utterance only after 40 consecutive non-speech frames (400 ms); any speech frame resets that silence count. Request analyzer finalization through the detected end plus 400 ms, then assemble finalized spans before exact-grammar parsing.
>
> Limit an utterance to six seconds from its first speech frame, including intervening pauses. At the limit, reject the utterance and suppress its continuing audio until 400 ms of silence; never accept a six-second prefix or a trailing fragment as another command. Accept at most one action per speech-session UUID and utterance number. Discard disabled, replaced, invalidated and expired sessions/results.
>
> Retain the exact existing grammar and finalized acceptance behavior. Discard results arriving more than 1.5 seconds after detected speech end and show retry feedback. Commands use the latest valid pointer at acceptance; do not backdate events. Require nearest-rank p95 of at most 1,000 ms from VAD audio-time detected speech end to native action acknowledgment, including the 400 ms silence wait, finalization, dispatch and acknowledgment. Report VAD frame quantization, internal hangover, conversion delay and input buffering; do not substitute callback arrival time or silence-completion time for speech end.

All 15 existing prefixed commands, punctuation/case normalization, synonym and multiple-action rejection remain fixed. Microphone remains off at launch and explicitly enabled; audio/text stay memory-only. The six-second drain rule makes rejection of an overlong utterance explicit without relaxing the limit.
